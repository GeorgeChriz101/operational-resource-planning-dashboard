# Executive Resource Planning Recommendation

## Executive Summary

The synthetic workforce-planning analysis indicates that current modeled staffing does not fully cover estimated operational demand across the 30-day period.

The baseline dataset contains approximately 38,552 workload units requiring 2,557 modeled staffing positions across all station-shift records. Scheduled staffing totals approximately 2,435, producing an aggregate staffing gap of -122 and an overall coverage rate of approximately 95.2%.

The analysis identifies 128 understaffed shift records and 82 High-Risk records under the project's coverage-risk assumptions.

## Key Finding 1 — Coverage Risk Is Concentrated

Dallas has the largest modeled workload of the three stations and also contains the largest number of High-Risk shifts in the synthetic dataset.

This suggests that resource-planning actions should focus not only on total staffing but also on where and when shortages occur.

## Key Finding 2 — Overall Coverage Can Hide Shift-Level Problems

The aggregate coverage rate is approximately 95%, but individual shifts can fall substantially below that level.

This demonstrates why an operations team should monitor station- and shift-level coverage rather than relying only on an overall staffing KPI.

## Key Finding 3 — Resource Utilization Is High

Average modeled utilization is approximately 88%.

High utilization combined with staffing shortages can reduce the operational buffer available when workload increases or unexpected disruptions occur.

## Scenario Analysis

The what-if analysis evaluates workload growth while holding scheduled staffing constant.

At the baseline, modeled coverage is approximately 95.2%.

With 5% workload growth, projected coverage falls to approximately 90.7%.

With 10% workload growth, projected coverage falls to approximately 86.6%.

With 15% workload growth, projected coverage falls to approximately 82.8%, which enters the project's High Risk range.

The modeled staffing gap also grows from approximately -122 at baseline to approximately -506 in the 15% workload-growth scenario.

## Recommended Actions

First, prioritize review of station and shift combinations that repeatedly appear in the High-Risk category.

Second, compare scheduled staffing with expected workload before each planning period instead of relying only on historical staffing levels.

Third, maintain scenario planning for moderate workload increases so resource requirements can be evaluated before demand changes occur.

Fourth, investigate whether staffing can be redistributed between lower-risk and higher-risk periods before assuming additional staffing is required.

Finally, monitor coverage rate, staffing gap, workload, and utilization together because no single KPI provides a complete view of resource-planning risk.

## Business Interpretation

The analysis demonstrates how a workforce-planning dashboard can help an operations team move from raw scheduling records to actionable information.

Rather than reviewing hundreds of individual records, managers can identify staffing gaps, compare station performance, monitor utilization, locate high-risk shifts, and evaluate possible workload-growth scenarios from a consolidated reporting view.
